Proxies without the black box.

Verdy Proxy is a proxy provider in public beta, built by people who were tired of providers that hide their network, their billing logic, and their failures.

Company story

Verdy started in 2026 with a simple observation: buying proxies meant trusting a dashboard you couldn't audit, paying invoices you couldn't verify, and debugging failures nobody would explain. We built the service we wanted to buy — per-country infrastructure you can probe yourself, billing where every charge maps to a numbered order and invoice, and a status page that reports real sync health instead of a permanent green dot.

We are in public beta: the full customer journey works end to end, and we publish what is finished alongside what is still coming.

Mission

Make proxy infrastructure boring to buy: clear products, honest pricing, automatic provisioning, and support that answers with data.

Vision

A proxy network where customers never have to ask "is it down, or is it me?" — because the answer is always visible.

Why Verdy exists

Because developers and researchers need exit IPs they can reason about: known countries, known types, known renewal dates, and credentials that stop working exactly when the subscription lapses — no mystery charges, no silent cutoffs.

Core values

Evidence over claims

No fake uptime percentages, no invented customer counts, no testimonials we can't source. Unknown metrics are labeled "not yet measured".

Automation over tickets

Verified payments provision in seconds without human approval. Manual steps are a bug report, not a workflow.

Least privilege

Developer keys use proxy credentials, never system keys. Admin tooling is role-gated and every sensitive action is audit-logged.

Philosophies & commitments

Network philosophy

One country, one accountable node fleet. Every node pulls the same allow-list every five minutes and reports its sync time, which we publish. Capacity is real seats, not oversold promises — full countries refuse new assignments instead of degrading everyone.

Security philosophy

Dependency-free control plane to shrink supply-chain surface, signed webhooks before any money moves, replay protection on every callback, rate-limited auth, and cutoff-on-expiry enforced at the edge. See the Trust Center.

Customer commitment

Support tickets with tracked status, invoices for every charge, renewals that never steal paid time (extensions stack from the later of now or expiry), and refunds through a real recorded process.

Infrastructure principles

Small static binaries at the edge, boring SQLite-backed control plane with real backups, health checks that actually check, and runbooks for node failure that have been drilled, not just written.

Transparency commitment

Public status with live sync freshness, incident history we don't edit, pricing that shows its multipliers, and a changelog for the service itself.
